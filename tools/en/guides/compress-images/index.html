Compress an image without losing the details that matter

A repeatable workflow for balancing dimensions, quality and file size.

Studio Irth · 12 October 2026

Separate file size from dimensions

File size is measured in kilobytes or megabytes. Dimensions are the number of pixels across and down. Resizing reduces the number of pixels. Lowering quality in a lossy format reduces the information stored in the image. You can combine both, but you still need to inspect the result visually.

Start with a copy of your original

Keep an original outside your editing session. Irth Tools creates a new download and does not overwrite the source on your device. Choose a still JPG, PNG or WebP image up to 20 MB and 40 megapixels. If it does not open, check the actual format: renaming HEIC to JPG does not convert its contents.

Choose the largest dimensions you need

Enter a maximum width and height. The tool fits your image within both limits, preserves its proportions and never enlarges a smaller image. For example, a 4,000 × 3,000 image inside a 1,600 × 1,600 limit becomes 1,600 × 1,200. Requesting a differently shaped boundary does not crop the picture. Use an image editor when you need a deliberate crop.

Adjust quality and compare

Start with an experimental value such as 80, then compare higher and lower settings. This number is an encoder setting, not the percentage of detail preserved. Inspect edges, lettering and smooth colour gradients. Small text may need a different setting from a photograph of the sky. Format, image content and browser encoding also affect the final size.

If the exported file becomes larger

The original may already be well optimised, or your chosen format may be a poor fit. Our tool shows both sizes and the actual change, including a larger output. A new filename is not proof of successful compression. Try WebP, smaller dimensions or lower quality, or keep the original when it is better.

A final publishing check

Open the downloaded file and check your main subject, background, colours and dimensions. When adding it to a website, write useful alternative text that describes what matters in context rather than repeating search keywords. Use a descriptive filename and retain an editable original.
