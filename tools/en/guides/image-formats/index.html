JPG vs PNG vs WebP: choose for the image

A practical guide to photographs, transparent logos and screenshots.

Studio Irth · 12 October 2026

Start with the job the image needs to do

The best web image is not always the smallest file you can produce. A product photograph needs clear detail, a logo needs clean edges, and a screenshot needs readable text. Decide where the image will appear and what must remain legible. Then choose dimensions and format, and inspect the result at its intended display size.

When to choose JPG

Try JPG for photographs with many colour gradients. Lowering quality may reduce file size, but it discards visual information and can introduce artefacts around text and edges. JPG does not support transparency. Irth Tools fills transparent areas with white when exporting to JPG. Keep an original copy and avoid repeatedly editing and recompressing an already compressed file.

When to choose PNG

Choose PNG when transparency or crisp text and graphics matter. PNG compression itself is lossless, but resizing changes the actual picture and browser export may discard metadata. Large camera photographs can become heavy PNG files. In our converter, the quality slider affects JPG and WebP; it does not control PNG compression.

When to choose WebP

The WebP format supports transparency and both lossy and lossless compression. Our tool uses the browser encoder with a quality setting; it does not provide a guaranteed lossless WebP mode. Try it for web images, but check that the receiving service accepts the format. If your browser cannot export WebP, the tool reports the issue rather than silently naming a PNG file as WebP.

A practical product-photo workflow

Suppose your original product photograph is 4,000 pixels wide and will be displayed much smaller on a store page. Keep the original, then create a copy sized for the intended display and pixel density. Export a JPG and a WebP at a moderate quality setting. Open both and inspect packaging text and product edges. Choose the lighter file that retains the detail you need. No single quality value suits every image.

What conversion cannot do

Changing formats cannot restore lost detail or make a small image genuinely high resolution. These tools are intended for still images; do not rely on them to preserve animation, EXIF metadata, colour profiles or print metadata. Check the exported file when those properties matter to your workflow.
